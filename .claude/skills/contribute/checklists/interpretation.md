# Interpretation checklist

The same rules as the defensibility checklist: one question at a time, quoting their sentence, answered in their words. Never a rewrite.

1. **Matches the table.** Does each number in "What changed" match the key results in the file? Does it name each key result that moved, and say which didn't?
2. **Against the prediction.** Is "robust or fragile" judged against the prediction quoted in the file, which was committed before the run, rather than against what the result turned out to be?
3. **Flips.** If a key result flipped under its flip rule, do they say so plainly? A flip goes in the supplement whether or not the branch is adopted.
4. **The claim.** What claim in the paper does this branch bear on? Does the last section separate what the data show from what the claim says?
5. **What it can't tell us.** One branch, one data snapshot, one decision point. Do they say what this result doesn't cover?
6. **Words that overreach.** Causal language ("because", "drives") the design can't support, or certainty ("proves") a single branch can't give.
7. **Ties and thresholds.** Where a result moved with one firm or sits on a tie, do they say so?

You may explain what a number *is* (what the key result measures, where it comes from, what changed in the inputs to it). What it *means* for the claim is theirs.
